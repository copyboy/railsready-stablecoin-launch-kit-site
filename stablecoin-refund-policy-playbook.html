Stablecoin refund policy playbook

Stablecoin refunds are not just reverse card payments with a different rail. The team needs to decide who sends funds back, which address is used, how sanctions and risk review apply, how fees are treated, and what happens when a refund is not technically possible.

Refund boundary

Document whether refunds are sent by the provider, merchant, custodian, or another licensed entity. The answer affects address collection, risk screening, ledger posting, and customer wording. Do not ask customers to paste wallet addresses into unsafe public forms.

Policy questions

  • Which flows are refundable: checkout, top-up, payout, settlement, or off-ramp?
  • Can refunds return to the source address, a verified address, fiat balance, or internal credit?
  • Who pays network fees, provider fees, FX spread, and recovery costs?
  • What happens after wrong-chain, wrong-token, underpayment, or risk rejection?
  • What data must be retained for audit and customer support?

Support status model

Use a clear model: refund requested, under review, approved, sent, failed, not recoverable, or credited by alternative remediation. The customer should never have to infer refund status from a blockchain explorer alone.

Operational controls

  1. Require a support ticket or internal case for every refund.
  2. Keep provider event IDs and transaction hashes with the case.
  3. Separate customer remediation from accounting close until confirmed.
  4. Use approved wording for delays and unrecoverable transfers.

Launch gate

A team is not ready for live funds until it can demonstrate a refund or remediation path for at least one successful payment, one failed payment, and one amount mismatch.

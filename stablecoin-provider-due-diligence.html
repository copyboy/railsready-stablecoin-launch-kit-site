Stablecoin payment provider due diligence

Provider selection should not start with fees alone. A stablecoin payment provider touches custody boundaries, address assignment, KYB/KYC, KYT, refunds, webhooks, settlement timing, data retention, and support escalation. Those details determine whether a pilot can survive real exceptions.

Start with the service boundary

Document what the provider actually does. Some providers sell checkout links, some assign deposit addresses, some convert stablecoins to fiat, some provide orchestration, and some only support a narrow chain and token set. The buyer needs to know who controls private keys, who performs risk review, who creates addresses, who converts funds, and who sends fiat payout.

Evidence to request

  • Legal entity, terms of service, privacy terms, and supported jurisdictions.
  • KYB/KYC requirements, risk review steps, and KYT or sanctions screening process.
  • Supported chains, tokens, wallets, address assignment model, and webhook lifecycle.
  • Refund rules, wrong-chain policy, underpay policy, overpay policy, and dispute escalation path.
  • Settlement timing, payout reference format, fees, reporting exports, and uptime or support SLA.

Questions that reveal operational maturity

  1. Can every payment intent be matched to a provider order and chain transaction?
  2. Can the provider explain customer-specific or intent-specific address assignment?
  3. What happens when a customer sends USDT on an unsupported network?
  4. Can the provider send test webhooks for successful, failed, refunded, and risk-review states?
  5. Who writes the customer-facing wording about settlement delays and refunds?

Red flags

Be careful when a provider cannot document address control, cannot explain failed-payment handling, relies on screenshots for reconciliation, avoids jurisdiction questions, or asks the merchant to make regulated-service claims in customer copy. Those are launch risks, not minor onboarding details.

Decision rule

A provider is pilot-ready only when product, engineering, finance, support, and compliance can each explain their part of the flow. RailsReady's paid template pack turns that review into a repeatable matrix instead of another ad hoc vendor call.

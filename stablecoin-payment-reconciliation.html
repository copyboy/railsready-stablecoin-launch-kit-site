Stablecoin payment launch playbook

Stablecoin payment reconciliation checklist

Stablecoin payments do not become operationally safe just because the transaction is visible onchain. A production pilot still needs to join customer intent, chain activity, provider records, fiat payout, and internal ledger entries without relying on screenshots or manual guesses.

The four records every pilot must join

A stablecoin checkout or top-up flow should be reconciled across four records: the internal payment intent, the chain transaction, the provider order, and the ledger or payout record. If any one of those records is missing, finance and support cannot confidently explain whether the customer paid, whether funds were accepted, whether risk review passed, and whether the internal balance was updated correctly.

Minimum fields to capture

  • Internal payment intent ID, customer ID, amount, token, chain, and expiry time.
  • Deposit address, address assignment model, and whether the address is intent-specific or customer-specific.
  • Transaction hash, source address, confirmations, detected amount, detected token, and detected chain.
  • Provider order ID, risk status, conversion status, quote rate, fee, and payout reference.
  • Ledger entry ID, posted balance, settlement account, exception owner, and manual override approver.

Daily close checklist

  1. Export all created intents and provider events for the period.
  2. Match each completed provider order to one chain transaction and one internal ledger entry.
  3. Separate pending, detected, risk-review, rejected, refunded, underpaid, and overpaid states.
  4. Confirm payout totals against bank or settlement references before posting final finance reports.
  5. Review every manual override, especially if support credited a customer before provider settlement completed.

What breaks most pilots

The most common failure is treating "tx hash exists" as equivalent to "customer can be credited." That skips risk review, wrong-chain handling, amount mismatches, quote expiry, refund feasibility, and settlement timing. RailsReady treats reconciliation as a launch gate rather than a month-end cleanup task.

Use this in a launch review

Before production, ask the product owner to show one successful payment, one underpay, one overpay, one refund path, and one risk rejection across all four records. If the team cannot demonstrate those cases, the pilot is not ready for real customer funds.
